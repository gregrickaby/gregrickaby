## Technical specifications

I grabbed the following specifications from each respective website.

### Behringer B-1 specs

- Type: Condenser, FET, transformerless
- Capsule: 1-inch gold-sputtered large diaphragm
- Pickup pattern: Cardioid
- Frequency response: 20 Hz to 20 kHz
- Sensitivity: 20 mV/Pa
- Max SPL: 135 dB (148 dB with the pad)
- Switches: -10 dB pad, 75 Hz low cut
- Power: 48 V phantom
- Official Site: [Behringer](https://www.behringer.com/en/products/0504-AAB))

### Neumann TLM 103 specs

- Type: Condenser, transformerless
- Capsule: Large diaphragm, derived from the U 87's K 87 capsule
- Pickup pattern: Cardioid
- Frequency response: 20 Hz to 20 kHz
- Sensitivity: 23 mV/Pa
- Self-noise: 7 dB-A
- Max SPL: 138 dB
- Switches: None
- Power: 48 V phantom
- Official Site: [Neumann](https://www.neumann.com/en-us/products/microphones/tlm-103))

Would you look at that? On paper, they're nearly the same microphone: same pattern, same range, and within 3 dB on sensitivity and max SPL. The number that matters is self-noise. Neumann publishes 7 dB-A, which is about as quiet as a microphone gets. Behringer doesn't list one on the B-1's page. With a voice a few inches from the grille you won't hear the difference, but on a quiet acoustic guitar or a soft-spoken read, you might.

## Which one sounds better?

It's obvious the production room environment _sounds_ better. That has nothing to do with the board, and everything to do with the professional sound card and a room with plenty of acoustic foam.

IMHO: The [B-1](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DV5QTVT9?linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=79c4d35308206f5c38041de58a5cdaf5&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) sounds brighter and a bit "punchy", while the [TLM 103](https://www.amazon.com/Neumann-TLM-103-Condensor-Microphone/dp/B00AAO3FQ2?linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=988878e934ee9134868a3d9b76f8d808&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) sounds warm and more "mellow". Overall, they're not that far apart. (Really.) Their frequency response graphs are nearly identical.

How? Quality of materials and parts used in construction.

What does that mean? Nothing. A Pop DJ might want to sound punchy, while a soft-rock DJ will want to sound warmer. Do you want to troll the name "Neumann" to owners of B-1s on the Internet? Or do you want to have a fantastic-sounding microphone for about $100? In the end, it's all about your budget and application.

A co-worker of mine, John Garrett, does voice-overs for a living. He started out with a [Behringer B-1](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DV5QTVT9?linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=79c4d35308206f5c38041de58a5cdaf5&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl). He jokes about how that "starter microphone" made him a living for a few years. (later he purchased a [TLM 103](https://www.amazon.com/Neumann-TLM-103-Condensor-Microphone/dp/B00AAO3FQ2?linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=988878e934ee9134868a3d9b76f8d808&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl).)

John Garrett's and my advice:

- Don't worry about the microphone as much as the gear surrounding it.
- Buy a good audio interface. It will make your audio sound better long before you hook up the microphone.
- Use sound-dampening foam in your studio or room.

## Buying checklist for 2026

John and I still stand by that advice. What's changed is the gear. In 2013, "a good sound card" meant a PCI card and a rack of gear. Today it's a USB box the size of a sandwich. If I had to start over, here's what I'd put in my office:

- Start with a [Behringer UMC202HD](https://www.amazon.com/Behringer-UMC202HD-BEHRINGER-U-PHORIA-2-Channel/dp/B00QHURUBE?linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=0e9f5462fed8e287bdfa01f34ea1733a&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl). About $90, two mic inputs with phantom power, and it plugs into USB. Add a B-1 and you have a voice-over rig for under $200.
- Treat the room before you upgrade the mic. Foam, a closet full of clothes, or a moving blanket behind you. A condenser hears your room as clearly as your voice.
- Recording in a loud or untreated room? Look at a dynamic mic. The [Shure SM7B](https://www.amazon.com/Shure-SM7B-Cardioid-Dynamic-Microphone/dp/B0002E4Z8M?th=1&linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=6a124efefbc818cca8c8ef324b9c5d98&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) hears far less of the room than either condenser here. It needs a lot of gain, so pair it with an interface that has plenty.
- Want the radio sound in one box? A channel strip like the [dbx 286s](https://www.amazon.com/dbx-286s-Microphone-Channel-Processor/dp/B004LWH79A?linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=8afb36db87780f17d9f55c9a6bb23172&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) adds a preamp, compressor, and de-esser. The [Symetrix 528e](https://www.amazon.com/Symetrix-528E-Voice-Processor/dp/B0006HBP2O?linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=7711ead3c3cd2c1a3e8a2b6981f3d517&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) from these tests is still around too.
- Buy the [TLM 103](https://www.amazon.com/Neumann-TLM-103-Condensor-Microphone/dp/B00AAO3FQ2?linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=988878e934ee9134868a3d9b76f8d808&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) when the mic is the last weak link. If clients are paying you and the room is already quiet, take the leap. Just don't expect gigs to flood in because you own a $1,300 microphone.

## Podcasting with a USB mic

When I ran these tests in 2013, podcasting was just becoming a big deal. Today it seems like everyone has a podcast, and most of them don't want a rack of gear. They want one cable.

That's the [RØDE PodMic USB](https://www.amazon.com/Broadcast-Microphone-broadcast-all-metal-interface/dp/B0BQM4TKF7?th=1&linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=f464ec7799e30580a826d9a2bd47b3f3&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl). It's a dynamic mic like the SM7B, so it hears your voice and not much of your room. Plug it in over USB-C and the preamp, compressor, noise gate, and high-pass filter all run inside the mic. That's the same job the Symetrix 528e did in these tests, minus the rack. There's a headphone jack for hearing yourself with no delay, and an XLR jack for the day you outgrow USB and move to an interface.

Want the real radio sound? Walk into almost any on-air studio and you'll find an [Electro-Voice RE20](https://www.amazon.com/Electro-Voice-RE20-BLACK-Broadcast-Microphone-RE20/dp/B08P7QTZM5?th=1&linkCode=ll2&tag=gregrick0f-20&linkId=3264f7c75cb71dfc7a87426bd1190650&language=en_US&gaOptInStatus=true&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) on the boom arm. It's been the radio industry standard for decades. It's XLR only and needs plenty of gain, so pair it with a good interface or a channel strip like the dbx 286s.

And if someone on a message board tells you your mic is garbage because it only cost $100? Send them the clips.
