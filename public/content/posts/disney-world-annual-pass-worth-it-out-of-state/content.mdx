By 10:15 on the Fourth of July, we'd ridden Big Thunder Mountain, Tiana's Bayou Adventure, Pirates of the Caribbean, and the Jungle Cruise.

Then we got a Dole Whip and left.

On a regular ticket, We'd never have left. Park hopper tickets for the four of us would have cost about $670 a day, and I'd have dragged everyone around in the heat until they turned the lights off.

With annual passes, a hot, crowded morning isn't a lost $670. We went swimming instead.

![Greg, Chloe, and Wyatt, seen from behind, walking hand in hand through Liberty Square](./greg-and-kids-magic-kingdom.jpg 'Me, Chloe, and Wyatt in Liberty Square, June 2025.')

We're not Florida residents. We live in southeast Alabama, about six hours from Disney World by car (more if I-75 is having a day). We've held annual passes for five years anyway, and I've kept a spreadsheet logging the numbers.

The short answer: yes, if you'll go about 7 days a year with the Sorcerer Pass, or 10 with the Incredi-Pass (the only pass most out-of-state families can buy). Below that, buy tickets. If you visit once a year for a week, skip the pass.

## How much does a Disney World annual pass cost if you don't live in Florida?

If you don't live in Florida, most of the annual pass menu isn't for you. Disney World sells four passes, and as of October 6, 2026, here's who can buy what (plus tax). The new prices are from [Disney Tourist Blog](https://www.disneytouristblog.com/disney-world-raises-rates-on-annual-passes-for-2026-2027-top-tier-up-by-120/). Renewal prices, eligibility, and blockouts come from [Disney's pass page](https://disneyworld.disney.go.com/passes/):

| Pass        | New     | Renewal | Who can buy it                  | Blockout dates                  |
| ----------- | ------- | ------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Incredi-Pass | $1,749 | $1,499  | Everyone                        | None                            |
| Sorcerer    | $1,139  | $969    | Florida residents, DVC members  | Select holiday days             |
| Pirate      | $909    | $789    | Florida residents               | Peak and holiday periods        |
| Pixie Dust  | $499    | $419    | Florida residents               | Weekends, peak, and holidays    |

So an out-of-state family gets two choices: the Incredi-Pass, the top tier, which anyone can buy. Or the Sorcerer Pass, one step down, which Disney sells outside Florida only to [Disney Vacation Club](https://disneyvacationclub.disney.go.com/) (DVC) members.

We're DVC members, so we have the Sorcerer Pass. It costs $610 less than the Incredi-Pass and breaks even about three days sooner, so my numbers below are better than what you'll get without DVC. (DVC is a timeshare-style membership that costs far more than any pass, and I know that's not a normal starting point, which is why I priced the Incredi-Pass separately. Nobody should buy in just for the discount.)

> [!NOTE] Why does Florida get the cheap passes?
> My guess: Floridians can drive over on a random Tuesday, so the cheap passes fill the parks on slow days. The Pixie Dust Pass is $499 because it doesn't work on weekends. We can't drive over on a random Tuesday. We drive six hours, so we need weekends and school breaks, and those are exactly what the cheap passes block out.

## Disney World annual pass vs. tickets: five years of costs

Every year I log what the passes cost and how many days we used them. Then I compare that to what we'd have paid for 4-day park hopper tickets, since most of our trips are long weekends and our pass lets us hop. All the numbers are for four people, and I priced all four of us at the adult rate.

Pass prices go up most years. The Incredi-Pass was $1,299 when Disney relaunched its passes in 2021 and is $1,749 now ([Disney Tourist Blog](https://www.disneytouristblog.com/disney-world-raises-rates-on-annual-passes-for-2026-2027-top-tier-up-by-120/)). For 2026 we paid the old renewal price of $929 each, which is why that row says $3,957.54 and not $4,127.94, today's renewal price.

| Year | Paid, with tax | Days | Per person, per day | Hopper, per day | Tickets total | We saved |
| --------- | ----------------------- | --------- | ------------------- | --------------------- | -------------- | --------------------- |
| 2022      | $3,596.00               | 14        | $64.21              | $142.25               | $7,966.00      | $4,370.00 (55%)       |
| 2023      | $3,596.00               | 11        | $81.73              | $148.00               | $6,512.00      | $2,916.00 (45%)       |
| 2024      | $3,957.54               | 15        | $65.96              | $166.75               | $10,005.00     | $6,047.46 (60%)       |
| 2025      | $3,957.54               | 23        | $43.02              | $175.00               | $16,100.00     | $12,142.46 (75%)      |
| 2026      | $3,957.54               | 12        | $82.45              | $188.50               | $9,048.00      | $5,090.46 (56%)       |
| **Total** | **$19,064.62**          | **75**    | **$63.55**          | -                     | **$49,631.00** | **$30,566.38 (62%)**  |

**Over the last five years, the passes have cost us $19,065. During that time, we spent 75 total days in the parks. The same 75 days on 4-day park hopper tickets would have cost $49,631.**

That 62% is probably high. I priced all four of us at the adult rate, and kids' tickets cost less. I also compared against 4-day tickets even on our 23-day summer trip, and longer tickets cost less per day. The passes still win for us, but by less than that.

Our smallest savings came in 2023. We only went 11 days, and we still came out $2,916 ahead.

Our best year was 2025, with 23 days, thanks to a summer trip of nearly three weeks. That worked out to $43 per person per day, when a day on a 4-day park hopper cost $175.

> [!NOTE] How do you find your break-even?
> Divide what the passes cost by what one day of tickets costs for your whole group. (Party size doesn't change the answer: both sides grow with every extra person.) Our 2026 passes were $3,957.54, and a day on a 4-day park hopper for four was $754 ($188.50 times four). $3,957.54 divided by $754 is 5.2, so the sixth day was where the passes started saving us money. Every year we've had them, the break-even landed on day 6 or 7.

## The hidden cost of an annual pass

![A Maleficent annual passholder magnet](./maleficent-passholder-magnet.jpg 'The Maleficent passholder magnet.')

Here's a fair warning: the passes made us go more. Once the tickets are paid for, a three-day weekend feels "free". It isn't. Our Presidents Day weekend in 2025 still cost $1,605, and that was a budget trip for us.

The pass covers one line of the bill. Remember the 75 days we've spent in the park? Well, we needed a place to sleep, a tank of gas, souvenirs, and a lot of Mickey waffles. For us, the pass is the cheap part of a trip.

### The pass is a sunk cost

Before each trip, ask whether you'd still go if you had to pay for the tickets. If the answer is no, the pass is pushing you, and the savings in the spreadsheet are smaller than they look.

### The pass competes with everything else

In 2024, after a $2,500 Disney spring break trip, dance nationals, and a summer of camping, we cancelled a November DVC trip so we could save up for the renewal. Nobody forced that choice. The renewal was due, and the pass had a claim on the budget before the trip did.

If you're an out-of-state family, the renewal is a bill that comes due whether or not you go. Unfortunately, there isn't a payment play for out-of-state passholders like Florida residents. A bad year (a layoff, a car repair, a sick kid) leaves you paying for days you can't use.

### A rough way to count it

Divide the pass price by the days you expect to use it, then add each day's lodging, gas or flights, and food. Our 2026 passes were $3,957.54 for 12 days, or about $330 a day for the four of us. Add a resort room and meals to that and the real cost of a Disney day is much higher than the $43 per person in the table.

The $1,605 Presidents Day weekend is the number to hold up against your own budget. The tickets were already paid for, and it still cost that much for a standard room at Pop Century plus dining.

Don't get me wrong, the passes _are a good deal_. They're also a very effective way to get you to spend money at Disney World!

## Who should skip the annual pass

Buy tickets if any of these sound like you:

- You visit once a year for a week or less. Even the Sorcerer Pass needs about 7 days, and the Incredi-Pass needs 10.
- You can only go on holidays or weekends and you're looking at a Florida-resident pass. Those are the dates they block out.
- You'd stretch the budget to buy the pass and then not have money left for the rest of the trip.

## What the pass changed about how we do Disney

![Tara, Chloe, and Wyatt posing with a giant Goofy statue holding a surfboard at Pop Century Resort](./tara-and-kids-with-goofy-pop-century.jpg#right 'Tara and the kids with Goofy at Pop Century, Presidents Day weekend 2025.')

The money is only half of it. The other half is that the pass took away the pressure.

When we paid for tickets by the day, we tried to get our money's worth. We showed up when the gates opened, stayed until close, and got hangry somewhere in the middle. My notes from our 2018 trip say the low point was a Monday afternoon with "no plan" and 81,000 other people.

With a pass, we can leave. Here's what that looks like:

- Labor Day 2022: EPCOT was nearly empty. We were in and out in four hours.
- Fourth of July 2024: four rides by 10:15, then a Dole Whip, the pool, and a nap.
- Spring Break 2022: the first note in my "what went well" list is "Instead of rushing everywhere, we spent more relaxing."
- DVC Spring 2026: we slept until 10 or 11 every day and stayed until the parks closed. Best trip pattern we've found.

Going early, napping, and going back later has been in my notes since 2018. The pass is what made it possible to actually do it, because a skipped afternoon doesn't throw away a $670 day.

## Where the ticket prices come from

Disney prices tickets by date, so there's no single "price of a ticket". For each year, I used [Touring Plans' median adult price](https://touringplans.com/blog/every-regular-disney-world-ticket-price-in-2026/) for a 4-day ticket, plus the park hopper add-on, divided by four. Their older yearly guides are on the Wayback Machine, like [this one from 2022](https://web.archive.org/web/20220517151056/https://touringplans.com/blog/every-regular-disney-world-ticket-price-in-2022/).

![Wyatt and Chloe, seen from behind, holding hands as they walk through EPCOT](./wyatt-chloe-holding-hands-epcot.jpg#left 'Wyatt and Chloe at EPCOT, June 2025.')

That wasn't my first version of this table. My spreadsheet used to compare against a ticket price I'd typed in each year, and I couldn't tell you where those numbers came from. I figured they were one-day prices and that I'd made us look better than we were.

Turns out I had it backwards. My numbers were within $12 a day of a 4-day ticket _without_ park hopping, every year. Switching to the hopper, which is what our pass actually includes, made the savings go up, from $26,055 to $30,566.

Even if we'd never hopped a single time, the passes still saved us $23,881.

## Before you buy

1. Do the break-even math with your own days. With a new Sorcerer Pass it's about 7 days, and with a new Incredi-Pass it's 10.
2. Renew early. [Disney's passholder guide](https://disneyworld.disney.go.com/passholder-program/how-to-guide/) says DVC members renew the Sorcerer Pass by phone, and you can renew up to 60 days before it expires. A renewal starts the day after your current pass ends, so you lose nothing.
3. Skip the big holiday weekends (if you can). Presidents Day was calm for us in 2025 and wall-to-wall in 2026. With a pass you can pick the quiet weekend instead, if school and work schedules align.
4. Book park reservations when you book the trip. Passholders still need them, and [Disney's terms](https://disneyworld.disney.go.com/annual-pass-terms-conditions/) let a Sorcerer holder hold five at a time, on a rolling basis.
5. Budget for everything except tickets. The tickets are covered. The resort, the gas, and the Dole Whips aren't.

We renew again in 2027. And yes, we'll be the family leaving Magic Kingdom at 10:15.
